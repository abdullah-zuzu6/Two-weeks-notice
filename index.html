Two weeks notice: what 14 days really obliges you to do

A US custom, a statutory minimum in Poland, and a contract clause in the UK. Same number, three different meanings, and one counting rule.

Updated September 2026  ·  Reading time about 4 minutes  ·  Topic: Employment and notice periods

On this page

    Two weeks notice is a custom in the United States and a legal minimum in parts of Europe. It is not the same thing in both places, and confusing the two is how people end up losing a week of pay or a reference. This covers what 14 days actually obliges you to do, how it is counted, and the handover plan that fits the time.

    Where the two week standard came from

    In the United States, almost all private employment is at-will, so neither side owes notice. The two week convention grew out of payroll cycles. Most companies ran fortnightly pay runs, so leaving at the end of a pay period kept the books clean. The custom outlived the reason and now functions as a professional expectation rather than a rule.

    In the UK, a 14 day period is written into many contracts for junior roles. In Poland, two weeks is the statutory notice for employees with less than six months of service. So the same number means "polite" in one country and "required" in another.

    How the 14 days is counted

    Calendar days, starting the day after the notice is received. Give notice on Monday the 6th and your final day is Monday the 20th. Two working weeks and two weekends.

    Where it gets awkward: public holidays, booked leave and sick days all fall inside the period. A holiday you already booked does not extend your notice unless the employer agrees, and in most contracts the employer can require you to take or cancel that leave during notice.

    Check for a "receipt" clause. Some contracts say notice runs from the date the employer acknowledges it, not the date you sent it. If yours does, get the acknowledgement in writing on day one or you may have started a day or two later than you think.

    A handover plan that fits 14 days

    Days 1 to 3: write everything down

    One document. Live projects with status and next action, recurring tasks with dates, system logins listed by name (never the passwords in the doc), key contacts with what each one handles, and the things that only you know, including the informal ones.

    Days 4 to 8: hand the work over in public

    Introduce your successor or your manager to every external contact by email, in a thread they can search later. Move meeting ownership, shared folders, dashboards and any subscription registered under your name.

    Days 9 to 12: let someone else drive

    Whoever is picking the work up should run it while you watch. This is the part people skip, and it is the only part that tests whether the handover document is any good.

    Days 13 and 14: close out

    Confirm final pay, unused leave, expenses, equipment return, and whether you are being paid in lieu of any remaining days. Get the leaving date confirmed in writing for your next employer's start date.

    Pay in lieu, garden leave and holiday

    Three outcomes are possible once you hand in 14 days notice, and the employer usually picks.

    • You work the notice. Normal pay, normal duties.
    • Payment in lieu of notice. They pay the two weeks and you leave immediately. This has to be allowed by the contract in many countries, and it is taxed as employment income.
    • Garden leave. You stay employed, stay paid, and stay home. Used when you are going to a competitor, because you remain bound by your duties for the whole period.

    Accrued but unused holiday is paid out in most systems, calculated to the last day of employment rather than the last day worked.

    Other 14 day windows worth knowing

    Consumer law is where the number really lives. The EU and UK give a 14 day cooling off right on most distance and online purchases, counted from the day you receive the goods rather than the order date. Many software trials run 14 days. Some insurance policies carry a 14 day cancellation window from the policy start.

    These sit alongside the longer retail promises of 30 day money back guarantees and 60 day return policies, which are voluntary company policy rather than law. The legal 14 days is the floor. Anything longer is the shop choosing to be generous.

    Common questions

    Is two weeks notice legally required in the United States?

    No. At-will employment means neither side is required to give notice. It is a professional custom, and breaking it can affect references and rehire eligibility rather than trigger legal consequences.

    Can I take holiday during my two week notice?

    Only if your employer agrees. Most contracts let the employer require you to work the notice or to use up leave during it, and they can refuse new leave requests.

    Does the 14 days include weekends?

    Yes. Notice periods are counted in calendar days unless the contract specifically says working days, so 14 days covers ten working days and two weekends.

    What happens if I leave before the 14 days are up?

    The employer can treat it as a breach of contract. In practice the common consequences are losing pay for unworked days, losing a bonus under a clawback clause, and being marked as not eligible for rehire.
